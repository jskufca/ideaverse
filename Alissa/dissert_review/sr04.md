# Revision Request SR-04: Clarifying the Spatial Experiments

This revision is about making the interpretation of the spatial experiments as clear
and careful as the simulations themselves. The experiments are interesting and worth
keeping. I would just like the dissertation to state more precisely what each
experiment varies, what the resulting comparisons show, and what conclusions remain
outside the scope of the current model.

This is not a request for new simulations. The goal is to clarify the interpretation
of the existing results in Chapter 5 and carry that same interpretation through to
Chapter 8.

## 1. Greened-zone size experiment

When the greened zone becomes larger, the total city size remains fixed. This means
that a larger fraction of the city is assigned to the greened class, which has lower
intrinsic enticement and a lower rate of criminal return.

Please acknowledge this explicitly when discussing citywide averages. A decrease in
the whole-city mean is an important modeled result, but it partly reflects the fact
that more of the fixed city is greened. It should therefore not be described simply
as evidence that larger greened areas are increasingly effective in real cities.

The boundary-versus-reference comparison is especially useful here. Since those zones
are intended to differ mainly in their adjacency to the greened area, it gives a more
direct way to discuss the modeled local spatial effect.

Suggested type of language:

> Within the fixed-city configurations considered here, increasing greened area was
> associated with lower citywide modeled outcomes. These citywide comparisons should
> be interpreted together with the boundary-versus-reference comparisons, since the
> fraction of the city assigned to the greened class also changes as greened area
> increases.

## 2. One large zone versus several smaller zones

The three configurations have equal total greened area, but they differ in more than
the number of greened zones. They also differ in perimeter, boundary-zone geometry,
and spatial arrangement.

Please describe this as a comparison of specified spatial configurations rather than
as an experiment that isolates the effect of the number of greened zones alone.

Suggested type of language:

> We compare three modeled configurations with equal total greened area: one large
> zone, two intermediate zones, and four smaller zones. Because these configurations
> also differ in perimeter, boundary geometry, and spatial arrangement, the results
> describe the combined effects of those configurations rather than the isolated
> effect of zone count.

The current results appear not to strongly favor one of the three configurations.
Please present that as the main conclusion. Avoid statements that multiple smaller
zones are necessarily at least as beneficial as one large zone, or that the model
identifies the best real-world arrangement for a city.

## 3. Conclusions and practical interpretation

Please carry these clarifications into Chapter 8.

The dissertation can say that the simulations provide useful theoretical insight into
how spatial arrangement may influence modeled outcomes. It should not claim that the
experiments establish that small greened zones benefit real cities or that one
configuration is generally preferable for urban planning.

A good final statement would be:

> The spatial experiments identify model-dependent patterns that motivate further
> empirical and modeling work on the size, arrangement, and local effects of greening
> interventions.
