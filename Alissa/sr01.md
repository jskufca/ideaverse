# Revision Request SR-01: Clarifying Validation, Scope, and Contribution

The goal of this revision is not to weaken the dissertation’s claims or to suggest
that the models have not been validated. Rather, I would like us to state the type of
validation being used more precisely and consistently throughout the dissertation.

These models were not intended to predict crime counts for a particular city, nor were
they calibrated to a specific neighborhood data set. Their purpose is to provide a
theoretical framework for studying how greening, spatial placement, criminal movement,
and crime-related enticement can interact. For that purpose, it is appropriate to assess
whether the models produce qualitative behavior that is consistent with patterns
reported in the empirical literature. The external evidence is limited and often
qualitative, but it still provides meaningful support for the models as tools for
investigating plausible mechanisms and generating qualitative hypotheses.

The Chapter 6 parameter work should likewise be described as identifying reasonable,
literature-informed ranges for exploring the models. It is not city-specific parameter
calibration for prediction. The parameter choices and parametric study help us examine
whether the qualitative findings persist across plausible regimes, and they help clarify
which assumptions and mechanisms drive the modeled behavior.

## Revision goal

Please revise the relevant passages so that the dissertation consistently distinguishes:

1. **Analytical verification or internal consistency:** limiting-case analysis,
   equilibrium calculations, stability analysis, and checks that the mathematics
   behaves as intended.

2. **Qualitative validation:** comparison of modeled qualitative patterns with
   patterns reported in external empirical studies of greening, crime, repeat
   victimization, and spatial spillover.

3. **Quantitative calibration and prediction:** fitting a model to a particular
   city or neighborhood in order to forecast outcomes. This is not the purpose of
   the present dissertation.

The key message should be that the dissertation provides **qualitative,
purpose-relative validation**: the models are supported as useful tools for their
intended theoretical purpose because they reproduce relevant qualitative patterns,
while they are not presented as calibrated predictive models for particular cities.

## Targeted revisions

### Abstract

Please revise the validation sentence so that it states that the models are not
calibrated for direct prediction in specific neighborhoods. Then explain that their
qualitative behavior is compared with patterns reported in prior empirical studies,
providing support for their intended theoretical use.

### Chapter 1

Clarify that the dissertation develops complementary models for studying possible
spatial mechanisms of greening. State that comparison with reported empirical patterns
is used as qualitative validation, while city-specific prediction would require a
different, data-calibrated modeling effort.

### Chapter 4

When discussing limiting cases, equilibria, and stability, use language such as
“analytical verification,” “internal consistency analysis,” or “limiting-case
validation.” When discussing agreement with prior studies, describe it as qualitative
validation or qualitative consistency with empirical evidence.

### Chapter 5

Describe the baseline greened-versus-non-greened comparison as an expected-behavior
or implementation check, since lower baseline enticement is part of the model
construction. Then emphasize that the boundary-versus-reference comparison provides a
more informative qualitative spatial result, because those zones differ primarily in
their adjacency to the greened area.

### Chapter 6

Describe parameter selection as identifying reasonable or literature-informed ranges
for analysis. Please be careful not to describe these values as city-specific
calibration, while still explaining how external evidence informs their scale and
plausibility.

### Chapter 7

Describe the parametric study as examining the robustness and qualitative sensitivity
of model behavior across plausible parameter regimes. The ODE and ABM need not have
identical numerical responses; similarities and differences both help clarify the
roles of their respective assumptions.

### Chapter 8

Please revise the conclusion to say that the models reproduce selected qualitative
patterns reported in the literature and are therefore supported as useful theoretical
tools for studying possible spatial mechanisms of greening. Also state clearly that
further empirical calibration and testing would be needed before using the models for
city-specific prediction or implementation recommendations.

## Helpful language

Please use wording such as:

- “The model is qualitatively validated against reported empirical patterns.”
- “The model provides purpose-relative validation for its intended theoretical use.”
- “The selected parameter ranges are literature-informed and are used to explore
  plausible model regimes.”
- “The results are qualitatively consistent with empirical studies reporting...”
- “The model is not calibrated for prediction in a particular city.”
- “These findings provide theoretical insight and generate hypotheses for further
  empirical investigation.”
